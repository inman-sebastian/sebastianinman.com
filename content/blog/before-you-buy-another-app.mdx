The tech industry's answer to every problem is another subscription. Before you take that deal, it's worth knowing that the software you already pay for is quietly sitting on features you've never opened. Most business tools ship with automation built in; it's just buried a few menus deep, switched off by default, and never mentioned again after the sales page.

Twenty minutes of digging often turns up more than a new app would.

## Where to dig

Open the settings of the tools you actually pay for and look for words like "automations," "notifications," "integrations," "rules," or "workflows." Some reliable spots:

<CheckList items={[
  "Your booking or scheduling tool: automatic reminders and confirmations are almost always in there, often switched off",
  "Your point of sale: receipt follow-ups, review requests, and a mailing-list sync are common built-ins",
  "Your accounting software: recurring invoices, late-payment reminders, and bank rules that categorize for you",
  "Your phone system: business hours, auto-replies, and often the missed-call text-back itself",
]} />

Finding one of these is a double win. You save the hours the feature was built to save, and you skip a subscription you'd otherwise pay for forever.

<Callout title="Why this is my starting point too">
  When someone hires me, the first thing I do is take inventory of what
  their current tools can already do. Turning on something you own beats
  building something new, and it's a big part of how projects stay at
  small business prices.
</Callout>

## Where the built-ins run out

Settings pages have a boundary: they automate inside one tool. The moment a job spans two or more (a sale in the register that should update the books and the mailing list, a form on your website that should create an invoice), you're past what checkboxes can do. That's real integration work, and it's a different kind of project.

But that's exactly why the dig matters. Do it first and you'll know precisely which problems your tools can solve for free, and which ones actually justify bringing in help. If your list ends up with things in that second column, that's what [tool and system integration](/services/tool-integration) is for.
