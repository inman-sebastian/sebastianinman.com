## Your tools should work together, not make more work

Most small businesses end up with a patchwork: one program for invoices, another for scheduling, a spreadsheet for customers, email for everything else. None of them talk to each other, so *you* become the connection: retyping, copying, and double-checking.

Integration means wiring those tools together so information moves between them automatically.

**Sound familiar?**

- A new customer means entering their info in two or three different places
- Your bookkeeping never quite matches what actually happened
- You've bought software that promised to "sync" and never quite did
- Switching between programs all day makes simple jobs take twice as long

**What you get**

- A review of the tools you're using and how information should flow between them
- Working connections between your systems: enter it once, and it shows up everywhere it should
- Fewer subscriptions, in many cases: sometimes two connected tools replace four disconnected ones

**A good fit if…** you use more than two pieces of software to run your business and you're the one shuttling information between them.
