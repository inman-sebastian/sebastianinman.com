# Proposal template

The one-page flat quote. Fill every `{{...}}`, delete any section that
doesn't apply, and keep it to one page: the proposal should feel like
the website reads. Deliver as PDF attached to the post-consult email
(see email-templates.md). Prices come from the service pages; never
quote below the listed starting price without Sebastian's say-so.

---

# Proposal for {{CLIENT_BUSINESS}}

**Prepared by** Sebastian Inman · sebastianinman.com\
**For** {{CLIENT_NAME}}, {{CLIENT_BUSINESS}}\
**Date** {{DATE}} · good for 30 days

## The problem we talked about

{{ONE_SHORT_PARAGRAPH: restate what's eating their time, in their own
words from the consult. This is the most important section; it proves
you listened. No technology words unless they used them first.}}

## What I'll build

{{3-6 PLAIN BULLETS: each one names a thing they'll be able to see or
stop doing, not a technology. "When someone calls and you can't pick
up, they get a text back within seconds," not "Twilio integration."}}

## What it costs

**{{FLAT_PRICE}}, flat.** That's the whole number: no hourly billing,
no surprise line items. If I find something mid-project that changes
the scope, we talk before anything changes on the bill.

Payment: {{DEPOSIT_PORTION}} to start, the rest when the work is done
and you've seen it running.

## What's included

{{BULLETS: deliverables from the matching service page, tailored.
Always include the handoff: "A walkthrough of everything, so you and
your staff can run it without me."}}

## What's not included

{{1-3 BULLETS: honest edges. Ongoing subscription costs for tools they
sign up for, content they'll write themselves, future changes beyond
{{SUPPORT_WINDOW}}. Naming these builds more trust than hiding them.}}

## Timeline

{{PLAIN_ESTIMATE: "About two weeks from the go-ahead." Days or weeks,
not dates, unless a real deadline exists (an event, a season).}}

## What I need from you

{{2-4 BULLETS: logins they'll share, decisions they'll make, content
they'll provide. Keeps the timeline honest.}}
